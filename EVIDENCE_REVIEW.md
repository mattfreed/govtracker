# Evidence review, November 25, 2026

The review covered earlier collections, the November intake and both additional-supplied directories. The inventory counted 171,142 files occupying 95.76 GB. A file can be a source document, a duplicate download, a text extract or an administrative record. The number of files is larger than the number of independent sources.

| Material | Coverage |
| --- | --- |
| Standalone tables | 144,356,438 records parsed across 2,183 CSV, TSV and compressed inputs |
| Text and compressed streams | 136,359 streams read in addition to the standalone tables |
| Searchable PDFs | 20,525 read directly and 8,766 with companion text files |
| Office workbooks | 40,963,222 worksheet rows read |
| Archives and Office containers | 62,690 regular members checked across 1,310 containers |
| Access databases inside archives | 26,281,515 records in 187 tables |

Counts can overlap where a table appears inside an archive and as an extracted file. Text extraction makes records searchable. It cannot establish that a claim is accurate. Case findings also require evaluating the document, the entity, the period and the amount. For example, a contract ceiling authorizes spending and a voucher records a payment.

## Checks that changed the published account

- **A housing bridge loan.** The extension for 629 Post doubled its original term. The City’s November project update placed the expected construction start in early 2027. The case identifies the parcel and distinguishes the loan principal, the state award, veteran-designated homes and the overall project. [Case and loan documents](https://sfgovtracker.com/#case/629-post-veteran-housing-bridge-loan)
- **A 2014 contract budget.** The City’s legislative analyst recorded $4,493,413 spent in the first six months, $52,057,485 budgeted for the remaining term and $6,246,898 in contingency. These reconcile to the $62,797,796 ceiling the Board adopted. The department had requested $71,260,913. [Analyst’s report, pages 44–46](https://sfgov.legistar.com/View.ashx?M=F&ID=3184657&GUID=EC33BB48-DEBF-4BBE-AE1E-A636AAF177BF) · [Adopted resolution](https://sfbos.org/ftp/uploadedfiles/bdsupvrs/resolutions14/r0304-14.pdf)
- **November 2026 contract rules.** The vote and the effective date were separate events. The City Attorney’s November 9 guidance projected a December state filing. It explained the threshold, term, construction, amendment and retroactivity rules. The date of the underlying agreement matters. [City Attorney memorandum, pages 1 and 11](https://media.api.sf.gov/documents/Memo_re_Impacts_of_Charter_Amendments_in_Propositions_A_E_and_F.pdf)
- **Recurring grants.** The review joined reported grants by federal tax ID and exact tax periods. Six original returns record a three-party circuit in two consecutive periods. Those filings identify the filers and annual grants. Following a particular City dollar would require payment and project ledgers. [Case and returns](https://sfgovtracker.com/#case/cpa-resilient-back-office)
- **A federal payroll finding.** PRC’s fiscal 2022 audit could not locate time cards for 22 sampled payroll items in the HIV Emergency Relief Formula Grant. The following audit reports resolution. A later $3 million subaward concerned a program in the earlier audit. The case keeps the award amount separate from any questioned cost. [Case, federal filings and award](https://sfgovtracker.com/#case/prc-federal-payroll-documentation)
- **Federal transit reimbursements.** SFMTA reported netting ineligible fiscal 2019 draws against a later request. The printed total exceeds the sum of the two listed expenditures by $1,682, the amount of estimated interest. The final posted adjustment and federal disposition still require records. [FY2020 follow-up, PDF pages 104–105](https://app.fac.gov/dissemination/report/pdf/2020-06-CENSUS-0000233179)
- **Voucher count.** A source passage described 88 vouchers. The underlying ledger has 88 accounting rows and 87 distinct voucher numbers. The case and its citation now use the two counts separately.

## Source verification

Across 148 cases, the source inventory counted 3,121 citation entries. The initial quotation check flagged 205 local citations for a second reading or a structured-record comparison. All 205 were reviewed. Some PDF quotations crossed a footer or a table break, and some payment assertions required a department-specific query. Of 164 existing numeric comparisons, 147 reproduced within the stated tolerance or after the documented adjustment. The other 17 reflect documented differences in definitions, periods or source versions. The 74 public investigation questions distinguish 21 completed tests, 11 partial tests and 42 questions that still require records.

## Open evidence

Public source links identify issuing agencies, docket or filing numbers, reporting periods and direct downloads where available. Some issuers require a browser, a search or a public-records request. Availability checks establish whether a URL responds. Citations and case notes explain whether the record supports the specific claim.

Four encrypted state forms could not be read. Certain supplied attachments were empty or damaged. A later agency download recovered all 563 pages of the City Attorney’s permitting exhibits. The request index identifies 256 outstanding items, including 77 at priority one. Some spending questions still require invoices, grant subledgers, attendance records or agency determinations. Each case identifies the missing evidence that could strengthen, overturn or resolve its findings.
